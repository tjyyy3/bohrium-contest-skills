# Evidence tests for six review facets

| Facet | Compare these objects | What the finding must establish |
|---|---|---|
| Facts and structure | Objective in introduction, definition or unit in methods, numerical result in table/figure, abstract and conclusion. Check function of sections, including merged sections. | Trace the same population, quantity, reference frame, epoch, preprocessing and conditions. Recompute from displayed inputs, show equation and units, allow plausible precision/rounding. A meaningful gap in the question–answer chain matters more than a missing section heading. |
| Citation support | Break the citing sentence into testable clauses. Locate the actual referenced passage in the permitted source, including qualifiers and scope. | Record the two propositions side by side: supported, partial, contradicted, or unverifiable. A bibliography entry or paper title alone cannot establish the cited conclusion. A partial match may support detection but leave attribution open. An unavailable full text is a recovery item, unless supplied evidence itself settles the particular question. |
| Method/design | Target quantity, measurement, independent unit, timing, comparator, assignment and processing, selection/exclusion, calibration and blinding as appropriate. | Specify the plausible alternative explanation and why a reported control does or does not address it. Check methods, caption, appendices and limitations. Unreported allocation does not establish nonrandom allocation. A descriptive survey does not automatically need a randomized control. |
| Statistical reporting | Find sample sizes and unit of analysis; separate repeated measures or technical replicates from independent groups. Match estimator, uncertainty type and interval, test and comparison family to the claim. | Show reproducible arithmetic if inputs suffice. Compare like intervals and estimands. Do not infer a p value from interval overlap, assume SD means SE, or recompute a paired test without pairing/covariance. Check rounding and multiplicity only for relevant claims. Report missing precision with the location and decision it impairs. |
| Logic | Match hypothesis/prediction to what was measured, what the comparison can identify and what the conclusion says. | Find a specific unsupported step such as association → cause, bounded sample → universal law, absence of detection → absence of effect. Check whether a limitation or explicitly conditional wording already qualifies that same claim. Name the strongest bounded conclusion actually warranted. |
| Academic standards | Determine which policy or requirement the case supplies and its applicability to this study. Check declarations, data/code location, reused images and permissions as relevant. | Quote the applicable requirement and exact scope. Separate confirmed noncompliance, missing information and optional good practice. Without a target policy, do not invent mandatory headings or accuse the authors of misconduct. A missing declaration in a provided excerpt remains uncertain. |

For subjective judgments give the criterion, source locator, acceptable alternatives and why the current evidence is sufficient or insufficient. A suggested reanalysis identifies its inputs, output and revised claim; where those inputs are missing, request them without supplying invented values. A revision can resolve an inconsistency by clarifying the population or narrowing a claim without requiring a new experiment. When two sources conflict and neither has precedence, show both and seek the authoritative version.
